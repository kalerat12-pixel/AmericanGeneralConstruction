Cell Biology — Unit 3 Notes

- Cell membrane: phospholipid bilayer, controls what enters and exits the cell
- Selectively permeable: allows some molecules through, blocks others
- Passive transport: no energy required, moves from high to low concentration
- Diffusion: movement of small molecules like oxygen and carbon dioxide across the membrane
- Osmosis: diffusion of water across a selectively permeable membrane
- Facilitated diffusion: uses channel or carrier proteins to move larger molecules like glucose
- Active transport: requires energy in the form of ATP, moves against the concentration gradient
- Sodium-potassium pump: moves 3 Na+ out and 2 K+ in for each ATP used
- Endocytosis: cell engulfs large particles by wrapping the membrane around them
- Exocytosis: vesicles fuse with the membrane to release materials outside the cell
- Hypertonic solution: cell loses water and shrinks
- Hypotonic solution: cell gains water and may burst (animal cells) or become turgid (plant cells)
- Isotonic solution: no net movement of water, cell stays the same size
- Key idea: the membrane maintains homeostasis by regulating the internal environment of the cell
