In The Great Gatsby, F. Scott Fitzgerald uses the green light at the end of Daisy's dock as a powerful symbol of Gatsby's hopes and dreams. From the first chapter, Nick observes Gatsby reaching out toward the light across the bay, which suggests a longing for something just out of reach. The light represents not only Daisy herself but also the larger idea of the American Dream.

As the novel progresses, the meaning of the green light shifts. When Gatsby finally reunites with Daisy, Nick notes that the light has lost some of its significance. Now that Gatsby has Daisy in front of him, the dream no longer seems as enchanting. This moment shows how the pursuit of a goal can sometimes be more meaningful than achieving it.

Fitzgerald also connects the green light to the past. Gatsby believes he can repeat the past and recreate his romance with Daisy exactly as it was five years earlier. However, the novel makes clear that this is impossible. Daisy has changed, and so has the world around them.

In the final pages, Nick reflects on the green light as a symbol of the future that "year by year recedes before us." This line suggests that dreams, like the green light, are always just beyond our grasp. Through this symbol, Fitzgerald critiques the idea that wealth and determination alone can bring happiness, revealing the emptiness at the heart of the American Dream.
