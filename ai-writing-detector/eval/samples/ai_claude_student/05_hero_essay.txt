When people hear the word "hero," they usually think of firefighters, soldiers, or superheroes in movies. But for me, the biggest hero in my life has always been my grandmother. She never did anything that would make the news, but the way she lived her life taught me what real strength looks like.

My grandmother moved to this country when she was twenty-two with almost no money and very little English. She worked long shifts at a laundromat during the day and took English classes at night. Even when she was exhausted, she always made time to cook dinner for her family and help my mom with her homework, even though she didn't fully understand it herself.

What I admire most about her is how she never complained. When things got hard, she would just say, "We figure it out." That phrase has stuck with me. Whenever I feel overwhelmed by school or sports, I remind myself that she faced much bigger challenges with far fewer resources.

She also taught me the importance of kindness. Even though she didn't have much, she would always share food with neighbors and give advice to younger immigrants who were just arriving. People in our community still talk about how generous she was.

A hero doesn't need superpowers or a famous name. Sometimes a hero is simply someone who keeps going, takes care of others, and shows the people around them how to be brave. That is exactly what my grandmother did, and I hope to live up to her example.
