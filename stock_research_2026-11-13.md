### Honest verdict
- **On returns, the picks would have roughly tied VOO** over the past year, with more volatility from the MSFT slot. Past performance does not predict the next 2–3 years, and the XLV/BRK.B numbers partly reflect momentum that may not repeat.
- **What you actually get:** (1) international diversification VOO doesn't provide; (2) less dependence on mega-cap tech and AI, which is VOO's biggest single risk right now (Nvidia is VOO's largest holding); (3) tax efficiency from BRK.B paying no dividend.
- **What you give up:** simplicity, plus single-stock risk. MSFT and BRK.B can each fall 25–35% on company-specific news. MSFT, BRK.B and XLV's holdings are **already inside VOO**, so those slots tilt your weights rather than adding new companies.
- **Are the picks worth the extra risk? Partly.** VXUS is clearly worth it as a diversifier. The stock picks (MSFT, BRK.B) and the sector tilt (XLV) are reasonable, but **I have no strong evidence they will beat VOO.** If you prefer fewer decisions, a perfectly defensible alternative is **$1,000 VOO + $1,000 VXUS** (total: $2,000 VOO / $1,000 VXUS). That captures most of the diversification benefit at a very low cost (VOO expense ratio 0.03% [S34]).

### The chance of losing money
- Over 2–3 years, stocks can and do lose money. Broad US stocks fell over multi-year stretches in 2000–2002, 2007–2009 and 2022 (general market history, not from today's sources). On $3,000, a 25% decline would be about −$750.
- Specific near-term risks: a Fed hike on Dec 9 with more to follow; a disappointing Nvidia report on Nov 17 hitting AI broadly; AI supply from a ~$64B Anthropic IPO; Iran/oil shocks; Taiwan risk (VXUS emerging markets, TSM).
- Only invest money you won't need for at least 2–3 years, and keep a separate emergency fund in cash.
